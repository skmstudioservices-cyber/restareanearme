Find a good rest area on your highway drive — and stop safely

Where to take breaks on Indian highways: NHAI wayside amenities, food courts at petrol pumps, trusted dhabas, and how to plan stops that fight driver fatigue.

📍 Open near me in Google Maps

⚡ Quick tips

  • Search "restaurant near me" or "food court near me" on Google Maps while on the highway — look for places just off your route.
  • Take a break every 2 hours or 150 km, whichever comes first — fatigue is a top cause of highway accidents.
  • Prefer lit, busy stops (petrol pump food courts, NHAI amenities, well-rated dhabas) over dark, empty stretches.
  • Check recent Google Maps reviews for cleanliness and 24×7 status before committing to a stop.

🏛 NHAI wayside amenities

NHAI is developing wayside amenities along National Highways — at toll plazas and nearby plots — offering food courts, clean restrooms, parking, first aid, EV charging and sometimes rest rooms. Amenities are typically operated by concessionaires and marked along the highway.

Keep an eye out for "Wayside Amenity" boards near toll plazas, and check the NHAI website for locations announced on your route.

⛽ Petrol pump food courts — the reliable default

Major fuel retailers operate highway retail outlets with food courts, clean restrooms and 24×7 operation — many Indian Oil, HP and BP highway pumps now include branded cafes. They're well-lit, have security, and are the safest default stop on unfamiliar stretches at night.

🍛 Dhabas and local food stops

>

Classic highway dhabas remain the heart of Indian road trips. Use Google Maps reviews (look for recent ones) to find ones that are clean and quick. On busy corridors like NH48 (Delhi–Jaipur/Mumbai) or NH44, famous dhaba clusters make natural meal stops.

Hygiene tip: busy dhabas with high turnover are usually your safest bet for fresh food.

🛌 Longer rest options

>
  • Highway hotels — book short stays via any hotel app when you're too tired to continue.
  • Railway retiring rooms — if your route passes a major junction station, IRCTC retiring rooms offer cheap clean rest.
  • Never push through drowsiness — a 20-minute nap at a safe stop beats arriving 30 minutes earlier.

❓ Frequently asked questions

How do I find a rest area on Indian highways?

Search 'highway restaurant near me' or 'food court near me' on Google Maps. NHAI wayside amenities, petrol pump food courts and well-rated dhabas are your main options.

Does NHAI provide rest stops on national highways?

Yes — NHAI is developing wayside amenities near toll plazas with food, restrooms, parking, EV charging and first aid. Look for 'Wayside Amenity' signage.

Where should I stop at night on a highway?

Choose well-lit, busy places — 24×7 petrol pump food courts are the safest default. Avoid dark, empty stretches.

How often should I take breaks on a long drive?

Stop for at least 15 minutes every 2 hours or 150 km. Fatigue slows reaction time as much as alcohol.
